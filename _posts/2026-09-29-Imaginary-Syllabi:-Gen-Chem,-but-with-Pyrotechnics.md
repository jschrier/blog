**Premise:** Teach the entire university general chemistry curriculum but everything is motivated by pyrotechnics...

**Origin story:** An August 2026 [New Yorker article, "The American Art of Blowing Stuff Up"](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/08/10/the-american-art-of-blowing-stuff-up) inspired me to learn more about pyrotechnics.  While I wait for [Grucci](https://www.grucci.com/pyrotechnicianseminar) to get back to me, I got into reading Conkling & Mocella, [Chemistry of Pyrotechnics: Basic Principles and Theory, Third Edition](https://amzn.to/4yIoamy).[^1]  It's a light read (if you have a PhD in chemistry)---the first few chapters are really a motivated recap of general chemistry, and the later chapters...are applications thereof.  Hence the idea:  **You could teach the entire standard university general chemistry curriculum, entirely motivated by pyrotechnics.*** 

# Advantages

- Motivates stoichiometry and limiting reagents in a powerful way
- Atomic structure and spectroscopy (understand different colors) 
- Computing redox is highly relevant (use of pyrovalent indices)
- Transition metal complexes are important too!
- Phases of matter (especially solids and gases)
- Thermochemistry (heat, work, equilibrium)
- Kinetics
- Acid/Base needed to discuss possible side reactions
- Labs could be a blast!

# Weak areas (relevance is less clear)

- Gas laws
- Solutions
- Electrochemistry
- Solubility and precipitation
- Nuclear chemistry/isotopes (unless you include atomic bombs, which would be cool)
- Won't appeal to premeds



[^1]: I also had day-dreams of taking the graduate certificate, masters, or PhD in [explosive engineering at Missouri S&T](https://mee.mst.edu/degrees/)
